# MAGNUS — CORE PROBLEM DEFINITION

## 1. Why Magnus Exists

People often have a reasonably clear idea of the life they would like to create.

They may want to:

- become healthier
- become financially independent
- build a meaningful career
- have strong relationships
- learn continuously
- create things
- travel
- spend their time intentionally
- maintain balance across competing responsibilities

The fundamental problem is usually not the absence of aspirations.

The problem is the continuous gap between:

**what a person intends**

and

**what actually happens.**

That gap emerges because intentions exist at one level while daily life happens at another.

A person may intend to:

> become healthy

but their actual days consist of:

> meetings, food decisions, sleep, workouts, travel, fatigue and interruptions.

They may intend to:

> change careers

but their actual weeks consist of:

> work deadlines, interview preparation, learning, networking and procrastination.

They may intend to:

> build wealth

but reality consists of:

> salaries, expenses, EMIs, investments, major purchases and changing priorities.

The user therefore has to continuously perform a difficult management process mentally.

---

# 2. The Core Problem

The core Magnus problem is:

> **I have an idea of the life I want, but there is a continuous gap between intention, plan, execution and reality. I need a system that maintains an accurate understanding of where I am, helps me decide what to do next, detects when I am drifting, and helps me determine whether I need to change my behaviour, my routine, my strategy or the goal itself.**

This is the fundamental product problem.

Do not replace it with:

> I need a better task manager.

Do not replace it with:

> I need accountability.

Do not replace it with:

> I need habits.

Do not replace it with:

> I need an AI coach.

Do not replace it with:

> I need everything in one app.

Those may be partial manifestations of the problem.

They are not the problem itself.

---

# 3. Why Existing Systems Are Insufficient

Today, different pieces of a person's life usually exist in disconnected systems.

Calendar knows:

> where time went.

Task manager knows:

> what was supposed to be done.

Fitness app knows:

> what exercise happened.

Wearable knows:

> sleep and activity.

Banking apps know:

> financial behavior.

Notes know:

> thoughts and plans.

Journals know:

> reflections.

ChatGPT may understand:

> a problem when the user explains it.

But no system continuously maintains the complete relationship between:

**desired state**

→ **chosen strategy**

→ **planned behavior**

→ **actual behavior**

→ **real-world outcome**

→ **trajectory**

→ **diagnosis**

→ **adaptation**

The user remains the integration layer.

The user must remember:

- what their goals were
- why those goals mattered
- what strategy they chose
- whether they followed it
- whether it worked
- which areas are being neglected
- whether circumstances changed
- whether a plan is still appropriate

This creates cognitive burden.

Important problems can remain unnoticed for weeks or months.

---

# 4. The Four Questions Magnus Must Answer

Magnus exists to continuously answer four questions.

## Question 1 — What do I want?

Magnus understands the user's intended direction.

This includes:

- outcomes
- standards
- projects
- explorations
- priorities
- constraints
- values where relevant

---

## Question 2 — Where do I stand?

Magnus maintains an evolving model of reality.

It understands:

- current states
- observed outcomes
- recent behavior
- progress
- commitments
- evidence
- uncertainty

It must distinguish:

> what is known

from

> what is inferred.

---

## Question 3 — What should I do next?

The user has finite:

- time
- energy
- attention
- money
- emotional capacity

Magnus helps allocate these scarce resources toward the most meaningful current gaps.

This is not merely:

> Which task is overdue?

It is:

> Given what matters, where I currently stand, what is drifting, what is urgent and what I can realistically do, what is the highest-value use of my attention now?

---

## Question 4 — Should anything change?

This is the most differentiated Magnus question.

When progress is not happening, Magnus must determine which layer deserves reconsideration.

Possible layers:

### Behaviour

The user isn't executing the chosen action.

### Routine

The action repeatedly fails because the surrounding system does not fit reality.

### Strategy

The user is executing but the actions are not creating the intended outcome.

### Goal

The desired outcome may be unrealistic, conflicting, outdated or no longer wanted.

### Information

Magnus does not yet have sufficient evidence to responsibly distinguish among them.

Magnus should not assume that every failure requires more discipline.

---

# 5. Magnus's Fundamental Control Loop

Magnus implements:

**INTENT**

↓

**DESIRED STATE**

↓

**CURRENT STATE**

↓

**STRATEGY**

↓

**ACTIONS / ROUTINES**

↓

**EXECUTION**

↓

**EVIDENCE**

↓

**OUTCOME**

↓

**TRAJECTORY**

↓

**DIAGNOSIS**

↓

**ADAPTATION**

↓

**UPDATED INTENT / STRATEGY / ACTION**

The loop repeats continuously.

This closed loop is the product.

---

# 6. Why Tasks and Habits Are Not Enough

Tasks answer:

> What have I decided to do?

Habits answer:

> Did I repeatedly perform a behavior?

Neither necessarily answers:

> Did the behavior produce the outcome?

Example:

Desired state:

> lose 20 kg.

Routine:

> gym four times per week.

Observed:

> gym adherence = 90%.

Outcome:

> weight unchanged for eight weeks.

A habit tracker concludes:

> Excellent consistency.

Magnus concludes:

> The chosen behavior is being executed, but the desired outcome is not responding sufficiently.

Therefore:

> increasing exercise adherence may not be the right intervention.

This separation between:

**execution success**

and

**outcome success**

is fundamental.

---

# 7. Why Goals Are Hypotheses, Not Commands

A goal represents an assumption:

> I believe this future state will improve my life enough to justify allocating resources toward it.

That assumption may later become incorrect.

Users change.

Circumstances change.

Priorities change.

New information appears.

Therefore Magnus should never treat goals as permanent instructions.

If a user repeatedly deprioritizes a goal, Magnus must not endlessly reschedule its actions.

At some point it should ask:

> Is execution failing, or has the goal itself lost relevance?

This ability to question the goal is core Magnus behavior.

---

# 8. Types of Things Magnus Manages

Not everything should be modeled as a goal.

## Outcomes

A desired state expected to change.

Example:

> Reach 90 kg.

---

## Standards

A state the user wants to preserve.

Example:

> Maintain a strong marriage.

Magnus monitors deterioration rather than completion.

---

## Projects

Temporary bounded efforts.

Example:

> Sell Jaipur plot.

---

## Explorations

Areas where the correct direction is not yet known.

Example:

> Determine whether becoming a PM is actually the right career move.

The purpose of an exploration is learning.

Magnus must not prematurely transform uncertainty into fake goals.

---

# 9. Magnus Is an Intelligence Layer

Magnus should not attempt to become the permanent database for everything in a user's life.

Existing products already perform many specialized jobs well.

Calendar should continue to manage calendar events.

Fitness applications should continue to record workouts.

Banks should continue to record transactions.

Task systems can continue to store operational tasks.

Magnus's long-term function is to understand relevant evidence from these systems and reason over it.

Therefore:

> **Magnus is the system of intelligence, not necessarily the system of record.**

During the Telegram MVP, manual conversational inputs substitute for future integrations.

---

# 10. The Core User Experience

The user should not feel like they are maintaining an operating system.

Magnus should feel like Magnus is maintaining the model.

Ideal interaction:

User:

> Gym done. Was unusually exhausted today.

Magnus can infer:

- gym action completed
- exercise evidence recorded
- low-energy observation
- possibly relevant anomaly

The user should not need to separately update:

- habit tracker
- health state
- journal
- energy tracker

The principle is:

> **Never require structured input when Magnus can reliably infer structure from natural behavior.**

---

# 11. The Daily Relationship

Magnus has different responsibilities at different horizons.

## Immediate

> What should I do now?

---

## Daily

> What matters today?

---

## Weekly

> What moved, what drifted and what deserves attention?

---

## Strategic

> Is the system itself working?

---

## Long-term

> Am I still moving toward a life I actually want?

Magnus should not constantly operate at the strategic layer.

Most days should remain simple.

Strategic intervention should happen when evidence warrants it.

---

# 12. The Core Product Differentiator

Many products support:

**Goal → Routine → Tracking**

Magnus must support:

**Desired state**

→ **Current state**

→ **Strategy hypothesis**

→ **Leading indicators**

→ **Actions / routines**

→ **Observed execution**

→ **Outcome indicators**

→ **Trajectory**

→ **Diagnosis**

→ **Adaptation**

This is the critical product distinction.

---

# 13. Example — Health

Desired state:

> 90 kg by July 2027.

Current state:

> 112 kg.

Expected trajectory:

> approximately required monthly reduction.

Strategy:

> sustainable deficit + strength training + walking.

Leading indicators:

- diet adherence
- exercise
- steps
- protein

Outcome indicator:

> rolling weight trend.

Observed:

- exercise adherence = high
- step adherence = high
- weight trajectory = materially behind plan
- dietary evidence = inconsistent

Bad assistant:

> Try to stay consistent with exercise.

Magnus:

> Exercise adherence is already strong, while outcome trajectory remains behind plan. Increasing exercise is therefore not currently the strongest intervention. Dietary consistency appears more likely, although available food evidence is incomplete. Improve evidence or review the dietary routine before changing the training plan.

That is useful intelligence.

---

# 14. Example — Career

Desired state:

> become ready for a stronger PM/product leadership opportunity.

Strategy:

> interview practice + resume positioning + portfolio + targeted applications.

Observed:

- interview preparation repeatedly postponed
- work performance remains strong
- user reports increased satisfaction with current scope
- no applications submitted

Naive system:

> PM preparation overdue. Reschedule?

Magnus:

> You have deprioritized this goal repeatedly while your satisfaction with your current role has improved. This may no longer be an execution problem. We should revisit whether changing roles remains an active goal.

That is Magnus.

---

# 15. Product Principles

## Principle 1 — Reduce cognitive burden

Magnus should do more thinking than the user.

---

## Principle 2 — Minimize maintenance

Maintaining Magnus must never become another life responsibility.

---

## Principle 3 — Facts before interpretations

Store observations separately from conclusions.

---

## Principle 4 — Evidence before intervention

Do not create confident advice from weak evidence.

---

## Principle 5 — Silence is allowed

If nothing meaningful needs attention, Magnus does not need to manufacture engagement.

---

## Principle 6 — Progress over streaks

Consistency matters only insofar as it contributes to desired reality.

---

## Principle 7 — Challenge the system, not merely the user

Repeated failure may indicate bad system design.

---

## Principle 8 — Goals can change

Magnus serves the user.

The user does not serve the goal database.

---

## Principle 9 — Explain reasoning

Important recommendations should be understandable.

---

## Principle 10 — Be calm

No shame.

No artificial urgency.

No motivational spam.

No gamified guilt.

Magnus should feel:

- intelligent
- calm
- analytical
- constructive
- appropriately challenging

---

# 16. Product Anti-Patterns

If Magnus starts becoming any of these, reconsider the architecture.

### Anti-pattern 1

A giant life dashboard.

### Anti-pattern 2

Twenty-five independent modules.

### Anti-pattern 3

A habit tracker with ChatGPT attached.

### Anti-pattern 4

A chatbot that happens to remember previous conversations.

### Anti-pattern 5

A complicated configuration system.

### Anti-pattern 6

An AI that gives motivational advice regardless of evidence.

### Anti-pattern 7

A system requiring daily manual maintenance.

### Anti-pattern 8

A product optimizing engagement instead of life outcomes.

### Anti-pattern 9

One giant autonomous agent with uncontrolled access to state.

### Anti-pattern 10

Treating every aspiration as a measurable KPI.

---

# 17. The Ultimate Magnus Test

Any mature version of Magnus should eventually pass this test:

> If the user tells Magnus almost nothing for several days but continues living normally through connected systems, can Magnus still maintain a useful understanding of what is happening?

And:

> When Magnus interrupts the user, does the user usually think, “That was worth telling me”?

If yes:

Magnus is functioning as an intelligence layer.

If no:

Magnus has become another system the user has to manage.

---

# 18. One-Sentence Product Definition

> **Magnus is an adaptive personal intelligence system that continuously understands the life a person wants, the reality they are living, the strategy connecting the two, and helps them decide what to do or change when those paths diverge.**

Every product and engineering decision should remain consistent with this definition.
